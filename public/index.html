What I do

Five jobs, all on contract, aimed at teams that already have strong engineers and want the pipeline around them to work as hard as they do. Most start with a look at what is there, because the right fix is rarely the one anyone first guessed.

01 / AI pipeline

AI pipeline audit and architecture

For a team that uses AI, or has been told to, and wants it to raise throughput without raising risk.

  • Map where AI touches design, coding, review, testing, and release, and what each use costs.
  • Find the gaps and bottlenecks, then plan the pipeline architecture and the automation that removes them.
  • Draw the governance lines: who owns AI-assisted work, and who answers when it goes wrong.

ProofCo-led a company-wide rollout of AI-assisted development: ran the pilots, wrote the evaluation reports, and wrote the AI usage policy. By our own sprint measure, one team's velocity roughly doubled after the rollout. Authored a phased agent adoption plan for a SOC 2-bound company.

02 / Delivery

Delivery, CI, and production audit

For a team that ships, but not as fast or as steadily as its engineers should allow.

  • Trace how code gets from a pull request to production: review, tests, CI, release, rollback.
  • Find what slows throughput, what makes CI unreliable, and what threatens production stability.
  • Written findings, ranked by what to fix first, presented to leadership.

ProofAudited a client engineering team's productivity and infrastructure and presented the recommendations to their leadership. Wrote engineering process policies covering pull-request and merge review, QA, production rollbacks, and Git hooks.

03 / Opening day

Launch readiness

For a product heading to market that needs the delivery side to be ready on the day.

  • Release management, checkpoints, and demos that keep a launch date honest.
  • Scope and price held to a cap, with trade-offs argued in the open.
  • A short list of what must be true before launch, and who owns each item.

ProofLed a three-engineer team that shipped three apps in six months inside a negotiated budget cap, for a launch announced at the Sundance Film Festival.

04 / Rescue

Hands-on fixes and rescue

For the problem an audit turns up, or an app that has gone unstable.

  • I can fix what I find, not just report it.
  • Stabilize with tests and standards, and leave the team able to keep it that way.
  • Take over a stalled build and hand back working software.

ProofStabilized a high-volume point-of-sale app after critical failures. Modernized legacy customer flows for a large enterprise client by adding test coverage and mentoring their engineers.

05 / Crew

Team augmentation and evaluation

For a team that needs another senior pair of hands, an embedded lead, or a straight read on the team before a big decision.

  • Augment your team with an engineer or a technical lead, including hiring and staffing plans.
  • Assess how a team delivers, and evaluate individual engineers fairly against clear expectations.
  • Advise on performance, promotion, and hiring, with the reasoning written down.

ProofLed up to 13 engineers through quarterly reviews and performance interventions, made promotion and hiring calls, and owned staffing against the contract roadmap. Ran up to five client engagements at once.

Rescue

The invoice that nearly doubled

A hand-off pushed a client's invoice to roughly double the original bid. I found the cause, corrected the engineer's practice, set up billing controls, and negotiated the resolution.

The situation

The client had a small budget and was billed ticket by ticket. An engineer who took over another engineer's work ran well over the original bid. The invoice came in at roughly twice what we had quoted, and the client was shocked.

What I did

I started by finding out why, before anyone argued about money. The cause was how the engineer who took over was working, so I corrected that practice. Then I set up billing controls for that client so a surprise like that could not happen again. Last, I sat down with the client and their project manager, negotiated a reduced invoice, and walked them through the reasoning behind the part that stayed.

How it ended

The client's trust came back, and it did not happen again. They went on to do two more feature cycles and later signed a quarter-long engagement.
